# M9 Next-Feature Selection Framework

## Purpose

Use this after the M9 decision gate, not before. It is a qualitative decision aid; it does not use a numeric score to claim certainty or create a market-validation claim.

## How to use the matrix

1. Cite actual evidence IDs, not a general impression or a template.
2. Describe the observed problem in neutral terms and include contradictory evidence or uncertainty.
3. State the current workaround before proposing a feature.
4. Identify privacy/trust consequences and course relevance separately from implementation appeal.
5. Define observable acceptance criteria; do not use “validated” as an acceptance criterion.
6. Record an explicit owner/team decision, including a decision to collect more evidence or make no product change.

## No evidence, no feature claim

A feature can be technically attractive, requested internally, or easy to implement without being validated or market-required. Without relevant, reviewed evidence, it may be recorded as an engineering idea or open question only. Do not claim it is easier, better, valuable, demanded, privacy-improving, or competitively superior until evidence and owner/team review support the specific wording.
