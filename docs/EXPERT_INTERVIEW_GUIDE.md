# Expert Interview Guide — 30 Minutes

**Use with:** career coaches, recruiters, hiring managers, or employability specialists.
**Purpose:** collect informed but non-representative perspective on job-seeker failure points, responsible guidance, and assumptions to test. Expert opinion does not substitute for target-user research.

## Opening and consent (about 2 minutes)

> Thank you for sharing your perspective. We are exploring a career-readiness workflow for job seekers. This is not a request to disclose candidate, employer, client, or organization-confidential information. Participation is voluntary; you may skip questions or stop at any time. We will store only anonymous notes unless a separate arrangement is agreed. We will show a fictional example and do not claim the tool predicts hiring or verifies skills. Is it okay to continue?

Record consent: **Yes / No**. Stop if no.

## Questions (about 24 minutes)

### Job-seeker needs

1. In your experience, where do job seekers most often struggle when tailoring a CV to one role?
2. What mistakes arise when they interpret a job description or describe their experience?
3. What creates avoidable time, uncertainty, or low confidence?

### Matching tools and responsible guidance

4. What can CV/job-matching tools help with? What risks or misunderstandings do they create?
5. What should evidence or explainability look like for a recommendation to be inspectable?
6. How should a tool communicate “unclear” or “no document evidence” without treating it as a skill verdict?
7. What safety/privacy boundaries would you expect before someone uploads sensitive CV material?
8. What should a private reviewer see, and what should never be exposed?

### Fictional EXE walkthrough

9. Review the fictional assessment/report/roadmap/draft flow. Which parts may be useful, unclear, risky, or unnecessary?
10. Does the source-grounded draft boundary reduce any risk? What risk remains?
11. Does private saved work, review sharing, or opportunity tracking introduce particular usability or privacy concerns?

### Value and affordability assumptions

12. What alternatives do job seekers commonly use? What would need current source evidence before making a public comparison?
13. What conditions might make a workflow valuable enough to use or pay for? What would make it poor value?
14. What affordability assumptions should the team test rather than make?

### Close

15. What is the most important hypothesis to test next?
16. What would make you advise against proceeding with a feature or claim?

## No-fabrication reminder

Record anonymous, factual notes only. Do not attribute a quote or endorsement without permission, invent expertise, convert one opinion into a market claim, or record confidential candidate/employer information. The guide itself is not evidence.
