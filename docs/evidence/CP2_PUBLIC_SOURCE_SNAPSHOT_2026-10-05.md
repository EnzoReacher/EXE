These are broad national context measures; they do not measure demand for CV services or willingness to use/pay for EXE.

Its extracted table note excludes universities and academies under Public Security and National Defence. Direct PDF retrieval timed out in this session, so owner source inspection remains required. - Public vendor pages do not establish actual uptake, outcomes, feature quality, candidate ease of use, willingness to pay, or market share. No checkout was completed. Tax, promotions, eligibility, and regional availability need confirmation where stated below.
- Register signals use H-COMP-01 for the narrow observation that public free/paid alternatives are listed, and H-CONTEXT-01 for national labour context. Neither is a hypothesis about validated product demand.

## Interpretation and limits

The source set confirms that free CV creation and free/credit/subscription entry points exist in the category. It does **not** show that EXE is cheaper, more affordable, easier, more trustworthy, more effective, or better than alternatives. The source pages are vendor-controlled claims; several pages have uncertain currentness or incomplete checkout/tax context. This source set also does not establish market size, market share, adoption, or product-market fit.

For the CP2 concept test, show a consistent fictional-data walkthrough and ask participants to compare it with the tools and people they actually use. Test whether the evidence-to-CV provenance and credential-backed skill gate solve a real unmet need, what parts are confusing, and what alternatives or price models they would choose. Do not lead participants with a claim that this workflow is unique or cheaper.
