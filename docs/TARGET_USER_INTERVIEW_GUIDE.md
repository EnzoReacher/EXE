# Target-User Interview Guide — 30 Minutes

**Use with:** job seekers actively applying for roles with an existing CV.
**Purpose:** understand current workflow, uncertainty, effort, trust, and reactions to a fictional EXE walkthrough. This is not a sales call or a test of the participant.

## Before the interview

- Use the fictional demo material in `docs/demo/`; do not request a real CV, live job application, account, or private review link.
- Assign an anonymous evidence ID such as `TU-001`; keep any recruitment contact details outside the repository.
- Record notes without names, employer names, email addresses, phone numbers, or distinctive personal details.

## Opening and consent (about 2 minutes)

> Thank you for speaking with us. We are exploring a career-readiness workflow and want to learn about your experience, not evaluate your ability. Participation is voluntary. You may skip any question or stop at any time. We will record only anonymous notes unless you separately agree to another arrangement. Please do not share your full CV, personal contact details, confidential employer information, credentials, or live application material. We will use a fictional example, and no job outcome is promised. Is it okay to continue?

Record consent: **Yes / No**. Stop if no.

## Current workflow and pain points (about 8 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last time you prepared or tailored an application for a specific role. What steps did you take?
2. How do you compare your CV with a job description today?
3. What parts take the most time or feel uncertain?
4. What tools, people, templates, or methods do you use now? What works and what does not?
5. How confident do you feel deciding whether your CV shows a requirement? What affects that confidence?
6. Without sharing private content, what kinds of changes do you usually make to tailor a CV?

Optional probes: “What happened next?” “Can you give a general example without identifying a person or employer?” “How did you decide a change was truthful?”

## Fictional EXE walkthrough (about 10 minutes)

Show the fictional flow only: assessment → evidence report → roadmap/draft → saved work → private review link → opportunity tracker.

1. In your own words, what do you think each finding label means?
2. What makes a finding feel supported, unclear, or not useful to you?
3. Can you find why the fictional report made one recommendation? Is the excerpt/caveat enough?
4. What would you do after seeing a roadmap item? What would you not trust it to decide?
5. How would you use or change the fictional draft? What would you need before using it in an application?

## Usability and trust prompts (about 6 minutes)

Ask participants to think aloud while moving through the fictional screens.

- **Assessment:** What would you expect before adding a CV and job description? What privacy information is missing or unclear?
- **Roadmap:** Is the next action understandable and connected to a finding?
- **Draft:** Can you tell what is source-grounded and what remains your responsibility to check?
- **Saved work:** Could you return to the right report/draft later? What summary would help?
- **Review links:** What would you expect a reviewer to see? What must remain private? Does revocation make sense?
- **Opportunity tracking:** Does “saved link” communicate a private reference rather than a verified or recommended job?
- Where did you hesitate, feel confused, or lose trust?
- What would make this easier to use than your current workflow? What would make it harder?

## Neutral value and price questions (about 3 minutes)

1. If a tool helped with the parts you described, what value would it need to provide to be worth using?
2. What alternatives would you compare it with?
3. What would you expect to be free versus paid, if anything? Why?
4. What conditions would make you unwilling to pay or unwilling to use it?

Do not propose a price, describe EXE as cheaper/better, or ask a participant to validate a predetermined amount.

## Closing (about 1 minute)

1. What is the single most useful improvement we could make?
2. What is the biggest concern or reason you might not use this workflow?
3. Is there anything we did not ask that would help us understand your experience?

Thank the participant. Do not request additional private documents or contact details for repository records.

## No-fabrication reminder

Write only what was actually said or observed, separated from the note-taker’s interpretation. Do not invent quotes, counts, outcomes, demographic details, willingness to pay, or product validation. An interview guide and empty note template are not collected evidence.
